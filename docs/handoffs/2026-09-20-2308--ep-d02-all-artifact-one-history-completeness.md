### A — every name mixes whole-biography and forked-fate artifacts

The set is empty. C129-C gives every name at least one complete “pilgrim”
artifact; A requires every name also to have an incomplete “forked-fate”
artifact. This is the strongest principled countercase because provenance,
commitment, and alternate destinies become mechanically real. Under one-copy,
no-transfer, permanent ownership, however, it becomes a mandatory copy-quality
lottery unless both incarnations are disclosed, accessible, and non-dominated.

### B — all-complete and biography-hybrid names coexist

The set is nonempty and proper. At least one name makes every represented
artifact one-history-complete, while another contains both a complete carrier
and an incomplete artifact. This supports systematic lineages beside
incarnation-sensitive lineages, but adds both a name taxonomy and a copy
taxonomy and can make all-complete names read as premium.

### C — every represented artifact is one-history-complete — recommended

`D^{all-Id-1H-allK}_{locked,v}=D^{U=}_{locked,v}`. Every represented artifact
has at least one qualifying history in which its complete form set materially
occurs. This closes C129's showcase-copy and permanent acquisition-quality
loopholes while preserving artifact-specific sequences, triggers, and
histories. Costs are a whole-biography authoring and validation obligation for
every copy, completionist “grand tour” pressure, and less room for deliberately
irreconcilable incarnations.
