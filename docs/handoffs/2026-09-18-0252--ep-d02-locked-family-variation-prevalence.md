### A — varying and stable locked definitions coexist

The actual-varying definitions form a nonempty proper subset of the union-
locked class. At least one locked name realizes a qualifying pair and a
distinct locked name exhaustively realizes none despite supporting one.

A's strongest case is a gentler content ladder: one-form locked covenants can
teach the class, while selected advanced names gain relational simulacra. It
also avoids imposing two-form authoring on every name. Its costs are another
hidden behavior subtype, unused structural capacity, and the risk that one
showcase definition tokenizes the class promise. Tight floors become
`7/10/60`: the extra supported-but-stable locked definition needs one key.

### B — every locked definition varies — recommended

Every union-locked definition realizes at least one same-`U`, unequal-family
exact-key pair.

B turns C93-B into a dependable class verb, earns C91-B's universal support,
and reinforces the **Souls and Simulacra** fantasy that one persistent identity
can have genuinely different relational forms. Its costs are a two-key minimum
and comparison/disclosure burden for every locked name, loss of the simplest
one-form teaching covenant, and premium-tier risk unless later materiality and
non-dominance rules create legible sidegrades. Tight floors stay `6/9/54`.

A/B are exhaustive because C93-B already makes actual variation nonempty.
Empty would contradict C92-B/C93-B; a numeric quota refines A rather than
creating another topology. Both retain Soul counts
`{6,8,9,10,12,14,15}`, attain tight degree five, and leave degree six legal.
Under A both tight-floor locked definitions are support-positive and degree
five; under B the sole tight-floor locked definition carries the pair.

At the fixed exact-key/Soul-roster boundary, fit remains direct. Each actual
cross-key family relation is partial; either prevalence rule is aggregate. B
is recommended for reliable gameplay language, not because catalog-wide
coverage makes the separate-simulacrum relationship more directly analogous
to *Achintya Bheda Abheda*.

Neither answer fixes exact-key pair coverage beyond one witness per varying
