### A — state-only comparisons only

`T^{1coord}_v={state}`. Because C111-C covers every completed key and no other
qualifying type exists, every completed locked key has a state-only partner.
Fixed-boundary comparisons are the easiest to teach, preview, and diagnose,
making A the strongest gameplay countercase. A removes isolated boundary-
expression comparisons; it does not mean boundaries never differ, because
diagonal pairs may still differ in both coordinates.

### B — boundary-only comparisons only

`T^{1coord}_v={boundary}`. Every completed locked key consequently has a
boundary-only partner. B preserves exact artifact state while its expression
differs at another evaluation boundary. Its best case is a conspicuous, routine
contextual-interpretation system. Its present risk is opacity because exact
boundary semantics, visibility, and player control remain unselected. B does
not make state globally invariant; diagonal pairs may still differ in state.

### C — both comparison types — recommended

`T^{1coord}_v={state,boundary}`. At least one pair of each type exists somewhere
in the locked catalog. C keeps both authored comparison languages in the
**Souls and Simulacra** repertoire without forcing either onto every artifact.
It does **not** give both types to every key, fiber, definition, instance, or
artifact. Its costs are two teaching and validation languages, token-minority-
type risk, and possible segregation by inaccessible snapshot, tier, or name.
Oppositely typed edges may share a middle key, so C requires neither two names
nor two instances.
