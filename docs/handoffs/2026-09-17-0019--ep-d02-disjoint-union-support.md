## Active owner choice — EP-D02-C3c.88

Let `R_v=V^{U=}_{fixed,v}` be the nonempty actual-rotator domain. Each member
has one forced union breadth `u_v(d)` and stable ambient noncore-palette size
`N_v(d)`. Define

`H^{U-disj}_v={d in R_v:2u_v(d)<=N_v(d)}`.

This is exactly the set whose geometry can support two disjoint legal unions
at equal breadth. It is support only: B or C adds no exact disjoint pair.
C87-A's capable nonrotators are outside the denominator.

### A — no actual rotator supports complete replacement — recommended

`H^{U-disj}_v` is empty. Every actual fixed-width rotation necessarily retains
at least one noncore motif while changing another. A degree-four Ashen can use:

```text
ambient noncore {a,b,c}; u=2
A1 U={a,b}  ->  A2 U={a,c}
```

This keeps a recognizable thread without requiring one universal noncore motif
across all forms; `{a,b}`, `{b,c}`, and `{a,c}` can overlap pairwise with empty
three-way intersection. A retains tight floors `6/8/48`. Its cost is that no
rotating fixed-width name can ever make a complete noncore metamorphosis.

### B — support-positive and support-negative rotators coexist

`H^{U-disj}_v` is nonempty proper. One rotating name is overlap-bound and one
could completely replace its noncore vocabulary; C87-A still requires a third
capable name that does not rotate. A degree-five support witness has ambient
noncore `{a,b,c,d}`, `u=2`, and legal disjoint unions `{a,b}` and `{c,d}`.
They need not be authored keys yet. B creates a fifth structural subtype and
raises tight floors to `7/10/60`, but preserves a possible rare “molting”
fantasy for a later behavior choice.

### C — every actual rotator supports complete replacement

`H^{U-disj}_v=R_v`. Every rotator admits a disjoint pair structurally, but none
is yet required in the catalog. C retains `6/8/48`, while restricting every
rotator to degree five or six with singleton count and map-group count both
two. It avoids B's support split but homogenizes rotating geometry around a
latent promise.

All branches retain Soul counts `{6,8,9,10,12,14,15}`. The complete legal
fixed-width tuple classification proves that only `(q,k,rho)=(5,2,2)` and
`(6,2,2)` support complete replacement. B/C require a degree-at-least-five
fixed-width rotator in addition to the inherited degree-five breathing lane;
no branch raises the tight maximum degree above five.

At the operative boundaries, fixed-key fit remains direct, support itself is
neutral, B/C catalog coverage is aggregate, and each authored cross-key
rotation remains partial. A is recommended for player-readable continuity and
taxonomy control, not because overlap is inherently a more direct theological
analogy. B's strongest countercase is a rare, disclosed, player-selectable,
non-dominated complete re-expression around one stable core.

A/B/C are exhaustive: `H` is empty, nonempty proper, or all of the nonempty
`R`. A prunes actual-disjoint behavior. Under B/C, the next overlap card asks
whether none, some but not all, or every support-positive rotating definition
