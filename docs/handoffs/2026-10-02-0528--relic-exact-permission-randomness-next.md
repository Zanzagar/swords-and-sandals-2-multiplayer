## Highest-value next step

Ask only whether complete intrinsic exact-permission laws can be genuinely
stochastic after all semantic inputs are fixed.

Let `W^{co-exact-perm-law}_v` be the nonempty set of canonical complete
intrinsic cross-active exact-permission laws. For law `w` and reachable exact
opportunity `o`, hold fixed ruleset/version, both Relics and relationships,
both selected ledgers, unordered Relic pair, repeated child, complete factual
context, and prior authoritative state. Let `pi_w(o)` be the probability,
immediately before the one designated permission realization, of allowing both
candidate uses. Define:

`G^{co-exact-perm-stoch}_v = {w in W^{co-exact-perm-law}_v | some reachable o has 0 < pi_w(o) < 1}`.

The options are:

- **A — deterministic intrinsic laws only. Recommended, with C as the
  qualified artifact-temperament/robust-risk-policy fallback.** `G` is empty.
  A law may respond to contexts, but a fully fixed opportunity always has
  probability zero or one.
- **B — every intrinsic law is stochastic-capable somewhere.** `G=W`. Each
  law has at least one reachable nondegenerate opportunity. This is
  universally stochastic-capable, not “every opportunity is random”; a B-law
  may remain certain in other contexts.
- **C — deterministic-only and stochastic-capable laws coexist.** `G` is
  nonempty and proper. At least one stable law class never rolls and another
  has a genuine nondegenerate opportunity. A single law that is certain in one
  context and random in another belongs to `G` and does not alone prove C.

A/B/C are exhaustive because `G` is empty, whole, or nonempty proper.
