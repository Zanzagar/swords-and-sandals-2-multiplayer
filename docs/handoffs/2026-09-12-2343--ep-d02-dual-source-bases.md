compliant entry and isolation counterfactuals proving that canonical action
semantics can change its basis match while an otherwise-eligible provenance-

## Sole active choice

C3c.31 classifies completed, versioned final effective input-grammar entries
after Relic, qualitative Bound-Soul resonance, and optional input Charm compose.
For each entry `G`, `S_action(G)` and `S_build(G)` are behavioral sensitivities
derived from reachable material isolation counterfactuals, never trusted author
labels. The domain `E+` contains entries sensitive to at least one dimension:
pure action `(true,false)`, pure build `(false,true)`, or hybrid `(true,true)`.
Basis-neutral `(false,false)` entry admission is explicitly separate.

- **A, recommended — single-basis only:** no entry in `E+` is hybrid. C3c.30-C
  therefore requires reachable material pure-action and pure-build entries.
  This keeps each effective recipe legible and mirrors one-sided Charm typing,
  but excludes recipes that genuinely need both action meaning and provenance.
- **B — hybrid only:** every entry in `E+` has both sensitivities. At least one
  material entry must prove each sensitivity through its own reachable witness;
  the witnesses may use different baselines and result directions. This creates
  maximum play/build integration but a universal specificity and teaching tax.
- **C — mixed portfolio:** materially functional hybrid and pure entries both
  exist. Which pure subtype or subtypes exist becomes the next dependent choice.
  This offers the most variety but the largest validation and balance surface.

The options are zero, all, or a nonempty proper subset of `E+` being hybrid.
Hybrid chooses no AND/OR operator, position layout, atom count, evidence phase,
or runtime/configuration rule. Basis-neutral admission, exact source families
and atoms, provenance keys, action phase, inactive/dormant visibility, arity,
dimension-to-position realization, reuse, relationship operators/timing,
Team Tactic/environment admission, payoff, compatibility, horizons,
persistence, validation, and release remain later. An owner-written replacement
may constrain hybrid membership through a disclosed future key.
