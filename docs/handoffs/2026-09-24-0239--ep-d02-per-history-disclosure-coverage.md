### A — every legal history exposes at least one form — recommended

`U^{pre-all+}_v=D^{dual}_v`. Every legal history of every dual tag carries a
qualifying P or R account before the relevant choice. A history may expose P
only, R only, or both.

Each account is **direct** at its exact `O^-` boundary. A singleton-supported
history's relation to the full dual vocabulary is **partial**. Excluding total
P/R opacity is **neutral/protective**, and universal tag/history coverage is
**aggregate**.

A gives every lived Soul–Relic biography a truthful causal foothold. Costs are
universal teaching/localization/validation, possible repetition, less occult
mystery, and token compliance through one obscure or shallow account on every
history.

Example: Ashen's covenant history explains P and R, its Ember history explains
P only, and its Echo history explains R only. No Ashen history is P/R-pre-
choice-opaque.

### B — every dual tag retains an opaque history

`U^{pre-all+}_v` is empty. Every dual tag has C159-A's both-form history and a
distinct history whose `K^{pre-hist}_v(k,w)` is empty. Other histories may use
any support state; B does not make every history opaque.

This is the strongest wordless-initiation countercase. Positive accounts are
**direct**; fixed-tag continuity between explained and veiled biographies is
**partial**; the opaque history's player-facing causal legibility is
**neutral/unsupported**; and universal opacity-bearing topology is
**aggregate**.

Costs are blind commitment, guide or data-mining pressure, route-dependent
comprehension, and at least two histories per dual tag.

Example: Ashen's covenant history explains both forms; its Veiled Ember
history has the same mechanically dual relationship but no qualifying P or R
account before any relevant choice. Every dual tag has such a silent history.

mystery premiums, and token membership through one obscure opaque history.

Example: every Ashen history explains at least one form. Dreamglass has a
Study history explaining both and a Veiled history explaining neither.
